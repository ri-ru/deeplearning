# Test Sets

This folder contains two human-curated evaluation sets that are used in
addition to the OOD test split inside `reformulated_full.csv`. They are each
20 rows; both target the same question — does the model judge equivalence
the way a human would, on inputs whose stylistic distribution is different
from the GPT-4-generated training data?

## `../synthetic_data_generation_improved/manual_labelling.csv` (20 rows)

Hand-curated by Flavia. Each row contains:

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| `Post` | A real social-media post (sourced from the HateCoT pool). |
| `Explanation` | The model-generated rationale for that post (the `explanation` column of `reformulated_full.csv`). |
| `Human comment` | A first-person comment Flavia wrote stating her own view of the post, in either an equivalent or unequivalent tone relative to the explanation. |
| `Label` | `Equivalent` or `Unequivalent`, assigned by Flavia. |

Per the project chat (2026-05-14): the 20 posts were chosen to mix obvious
and subtle cases, and the comments were written in a casual first-person
voice. Of the 20, 13 are labelled `Equivalent` and 7 are labelled
`Unequivalent`.

**Dual use — this is also the few-shot seed.** The same file is loaded by
`synthetic_data_generation_improved/script.py` (`load_fewshot_examples`) and
its rows are injected verbatim into the Qwen generation prompt as few-shot
examples. The synthetic training data is therefore stylistically conditioned
on these 20 rows.

When `evaluate_manual.py` then scores the trained RoBERTa on this same file,
it is **not** a clean held-out test. A genuine independent eval would either
hold out, say, 10 of the 20 rows from the few-shot pool, or label a new
batch from scratch.

## `claude_test_labels.csv` (22 rows in CSV, 20 evaluation rows)

Per the project chat (2026-05-14, message 9–10 of Flavia's timeline):
generated by prompting Claude 4.6 with "the same instructions used with
Qwen" — i.e. the `build_system_prompt_equivalent` / `_unequivalent`
templates in `synthetic_data_generation_improved/script.py`, applied to a
fresh batch of posts and rationales, then human-judged.

Reported result (screenshot in the chat, eval script not committed yet for
Claude specifically): RoBERTa scores 10/20 — perfect on the 10 unequivalent
rows but only 3/10 on the equivalent rows. Interpreted in the chat as
evidence that the trained model is stylistically biased toward predicting
`unequivalent` whenever the rationale "feels different" — the Claude voice
("This post is …") is different enough from the Qwen voice ("I find it
…") to trip the classifier even when the two rationales mean the same
thing.
