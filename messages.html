@dimitri_pelgrin as Veronika might have told you, we are exploring startup ideas. Hence, I though if we could find something that is closer to the startup space, we are exploring then we would be even more motivated for the project :) (we would do great job anyway but would help to go the extra mile).

I though about the following idea:
1. We use a dataset that already includes ground-truth text classifications of tweets or social media content into hate/non-hate or more subtle categories ideally.

2.We create a test set where we either keep the ground-truth label or replace it with a different one for each text segments, it is supposed to emulate the output of a model that a Data Scientist would like to evaluate.

3. We then train a judge ( LLM-as-a-Judge) to distinguish between the correct ground-truth label and the adversarial label. Once the judge is performant enough, it helps developers create an inexpensive judge that they can reuse on their own ground truth ( that they create manually themsleves for example) to evaluate a model outputs. I believe it is interesting because the judge could help them to stay aligned with their own beliefs and norms ( since they choose their ground truth themselves) and since they cannot always rely on human annotators, and because directly asking an LLM (without any ground truth) to determine whether something counts as hate speech is difficult.

Hello folks,
After looking for datasets given on Moodle, here are my comments:
- The TARGE paper (which is thus far the closest paper that I've found related to our work) uses the ETHOS dataset (given below): the first variant classifies HS in a binary way, whereas the second dataset also classifies the HS comments in categories. Note the small size of the dataset (~1K samples). Here is the link: https://github.com/intelligence-csd-auth-gr/Ethos-Hate-Speech-Dataset/tree/master
- The paper uses the HateXplain as a benchmark: the complicated part about this dataset is that the rationales are binarily encoded on a token basis (0 means the token is not harmful, 1 means the token contributes to HS). Here is the link: https://github.com/hate-alert/HateXplain/tree/master
- Another idea that come to mind in case our rationale reasoning stands: given the fact that Flavia's "hatecot_final_D3.csv" is composed of 8 distinct domains, we could maybe exclude one of the eight and keep it solely for testing. This simple method would tackle the problem of dataset bias that Veronika brought up last time, in my opinion.
